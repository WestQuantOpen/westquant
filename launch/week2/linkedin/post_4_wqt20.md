We're training a 20M-parameter Transformer on 100M quantum optimization cases.

The model is called WQT20. And the training data doesn't exist yet — because we're building the factory that produces it.

Here's the two-step story:

**Step 1 (live now): WestQuant Open infrastructure**
- Open-source Python packages on PyPI
- Generates ML training data from quantum compilation
- Searches representation spaces across Qiskit, PennyLane, Cirq
- Predicts QPU budgets before hardware execution
- Every run produces (state, action, reward) tuples

**Step 2 (in development): WQT20 model**
- 19.06M parameters, 8 layers, 384 hidden dim
- Standard LlamaConfig — no exotic architecture
- Trained on data generated by the infrastructure
- Learns to rank compilation policies: given a circuit and hardware target, which transformation sequence minimizes depth, 2Q gates, and error?

The model never executes transformations. It ranks legal actions from a verified registry. Deterministic math executes. Verification certifies.

Why this matters:

Current quantum compilers use fixed pipelines designed by hand. Qiskit has 4 optimization levels. PennyLane has a fixed transform stack. The order of passes matters — but nobody searches it systematically.

WQT20 learns what works. From 100M real compilation trajectories. Across 10 algorithm families. Across 5 hardware targets. Across multiple objective functions.

And the data factory keeps running. New algorithms, new hardware, new objectives — continuously generating fresh training data.

This is not a model trained on a static dataset. It's a model trained on a living data factory.
